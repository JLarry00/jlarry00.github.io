# Technical portfolio evidence

Make the portfolio answer relevant professional questions through real work. Prefer the chain:

`market need → recurring capability → verified evidence → project or experience → personal contribution → decision → result, limitation, or learning → portfolio placement`

Do not reduce the portfolio to a technology inventory.

## Start with purpose

Before selecting sections or projects, establish:

- why the website exists now;
- the primary and secondary audiences;
- what each should understand;
- what each should inspect;
- what action should follow;
- privacy and publication boundaries.

If these are unsettled, return them as proposals and pending decisions. Do not silently decide them through page structure.

## Analyze market evidence when needed

When the portfolio needs market alignment, analyze a dated sample of several live, representative opportunities rather than one job description. Prefer primary vacancy pages with functioning application flows. Deduplicate company, role, and URL.

For a broad AI engineering direction, sample relevant adjacent families such as AI Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, Agentic AI Engineer, AI Platform or infrastructure roles, and backend or platform roles with agent-system responsibilities. Adapt the families to the user's objective.

Cluster recurring capabilities by underlying engineering problem. Separate:

- repeated signals;
- role- or company-specific signals;
- seniority-dependent expectations;
- volatile tools and frameworks;
- inferred signals that require caution.

Read [market-evidence-to-portfolio.md](references/market-evidence-to-portfolio.md) before producing the matrix. Date all market conclusions and state sample limitations.

## Map capabilities to evidence

For every relevant capability, capture:

| Capability | Evidence | Project or experience | Personal contribution | Decision or tradeoff | Result, limitation, or learning | Publicability | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Distinguish demonstrated capability, partial evidence, professional direction, and unsupported claim. One artifact may support several capabilities, but do not multiply the same evidence into an illusion of breadth.

When attribution or maturity is unclear, use the evidence-mapping steps in this skill to flag what is known, what is unverified, and what source would resolve the gap.

## Select portfolio proof

Choose a small set of projects or experiences that jointly show:

- relevance to the intended opportunity;
- meaningful personal decisions;
- technical depth appropriate to the level;
- verification and failure awareness;
- complementarity rather than repetition;
- enough public evidence to inspect safely.

Do not rank projects by recency alone. Do not require every technology or role expectation to appear. Give each selected item a specific job in the portfolio.

For each item define:

1. audience question it answers;
2. claim it supports;
3. exact maturity and ownership;
4. strongest evidence;
5. key decision or tradeoff;
6. result, limitation, or learning;
7. appropriate depth and destination;
8. what should remain private.

## Propose content architecture

Turn the selected evidence into an ordered content hypothesis. A typical portfolio may need a focused first screen, selected work, routes to technical cases, concise professional context, and contact links. Include a complete chronology only when the audience requires it.

Explain why each element exists and which audience question it answers. Leave visual treatment to the installed `professional-homepage-direction` skill.

## Apply the technical discovery baseline

When implementation or publication planning reaches technical SEO, read [technical-seo-baseline.md](references/technical-seo-baseline.md). Separate:

- established web and search practices;
- context-dependent options;
- experimental agent-discovery proposals.

Never claim guaranteed indexing, ranking, rich results, entity recognition, LLM citation, or recruiter discovery.

## Deliverables

Return the relevant subset:

1. purpose and audience decisions;
2. dated market sample and limitations;
3. recurring capability clusters;
4. capability-to-evidence matrix;
5. selected proof units and rejected alternatives;
6. content architecture and rationale;
7. evidence gaps and next proof to build;
8. stable SEO checklist and experimental options;
9. confirmed facts, proposals, pending decisions, and private inputs.

## Release gate

Reject the result when:

- it mirrors one vacancy or reads like an ATS document;
- technologies replace problems, decisions, and outcomes;
- a capability has no evidence or accurate direction label;
- projects are selected because they are recent or visually convenient;
- content architecture silently settles an unresolved positioning decision;
- third-party or private information is exposed;
- SEO advice promises an outcome it cannot guarantee;
- `llms.txt`, structured data, or `sameAs` is presented as a ranking shortcut.
